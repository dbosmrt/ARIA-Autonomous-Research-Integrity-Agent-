## Genetic  fidelity  using  molecular  marker  and in  vitro regeneration  of Meizotropis  pellita :  an  endangered  and endemic forest plant species of Uttarakhand

Sneha Bhandari * and Tapan K. Nailwal Department  of  Biotechnology,  Kumaun  University,  Bhimtal Campus, Bhimtal, Nainital -263136 snehabhandari43@gmail.com

Abstract -Meizotropis pellita ,  popularly  known as 'Patwa' in local dialect, is an endangered plant species of Uttarakhand. It belongs to the bean family i.e. the Fabaceae or Leguminosae. This angiosperm species  is  at  the  verge  of  extinction  due  to  large-scale  human interference, increasing urbanization, extreme deforestation, intruding wild species reduces the habitat of native species. Present study is based on the mass multiplication of M. pellita , in vitro and its  genetic  fidelity  analysis  with  wild  species  using  RAPD  as molecular marker. Genetic solidarity of the plant progeny regenerated via micropropagation becomes crucial if genetic transformation studies have to be carried out. Somaclonal variation in  micropropagation  procedures  is  a  frequent  phenomenon  which makes  it  obligatory  to  examine    genetic  stability  of  plants.  After successful  regeneration  of  plant  using  seeds,  apical  nodes  and internodes  were  taken  as  explants  for  further  shoot  and  root regeneration. 6-Benzylaminopurine (17.6 μM) + Giberellic acid (1.0 μM) for shoot regeneration and Indole-3-butyric acid (4.9 μM) for root regeneration were found out to be most effective hormones in plant  micro-propagation.  Genetic  diversity  analysis  was  done  by comparing the extracted DNA from both in vitro micro propagated plants with those of wild plant using Random Amplified Polymorphic DNA (RAPD) as marker.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Meizotropis pellita (Wall. Ex Hook, F and Grev.) [1] commonly  known  as  Patwa,  is  native  species  of  Patwadanger downhill  from  Nainital  in  Uttarakhand.  Osmoston,  a  British botanist  first  reported  Patwa  in  1925  at  Patwadanger  (1530  m) above sea level. There are just 50-100 plants present at Patwadanger    Growth  occurs  in  the  months  of  April  to  May. This angiosperm plant species is at the verge of extinction due to large-scale human  interference, increasing urbanization and extreme deforestation.

Corresponding author *

To protect its existence in nature, plant tissue culture is one of the best way to conserve this species. For the protection of  biodiversity    tissue  culture  is  used  for  conservation  of biological  diversity  and  threatened  species  that  significantly have small communities, for species with progenitive complications and for rehabilitation and reintroduction (Bramwell,  1990)  [2]. Genetic  consistency  and  stability  of the tissue culture regenerated plantlets is a critical specification  for  tissue  culture  procedures.  In  spite  of  the fact that in  vitro germinated  plants  sustain  the  genetic fidelity, still the likelihood of producing somaclonal variation subsists (Bindiya and Kanwar, 2003) [3]. Various molecular markers can be used to assess the genetic fidelity of in vitro derived plants. However, random amplified  polymorphic  DNA  (RAPD)  is  methodical  and cost-effective  among  numerous  markers (Jayanthi  and Mandal, 2001)  [4]. So,  RAPD  was  used  as  molecular marker in order to analyze the genetic fidelity assessment in micropropagated plants with those of wild plants of Meizotropis pellita .

## II. MATERIALS AND METHODS

## A. Sterilization and inoculation of seeds:

Seeds  from  wild  were  taken  for in  vitro germination  of Patwa.  After  removal  of  seed  coat,  seeds  were  washed thoroughly under tap water for 20 min followed by immersion in Bavistin (1%) solution for 20-30 min. Seeds were  washed  repeatedly  with  distilled  water  for  proper removal  of    Bavistin  from  them  and  treated  with  0.1% Tween  20  for  5  to  10  min  followed  by  repeated  washing with  distilled  water.  Seeds  were  treated  with  70%  ethanol for  1  min  inside  laminar  airflow  chamber  and  then  with 0.1%  HgCl2 (w/v)  followed  by  repeated  washing  with autoclaved  double  distilled  water  to  ensure  removal  of traces  of  chemical,  if  present  after  treatment. ## B. Callus induction:

The nodes and young leaves of in vitro germinated plants from seeds  were  taken  as  explants  for  callus  induction.  Leaves  and nodes were excised (1-2 cm 2 ) from in vitro germinated plant and were inoculated in callus induction medium, supplemented with either Auxin ; 2,4-D alone or in combination with Cytokinin ; 2iP, respectively.

## C. Shoot induction:

Induced  callus  were  inoculated  in  shoot  induction  medium  in culture  bottles  which  was  supplemented  with  different  growth hormones viz., BAP in combination with GA 3 .

## D. Root induction:

In-vitro generated shoots (2-4 cm in length) were inoculated in root induction medium supplemented with IBA at a concentration of 4.9 μM for regeneration of roots.

## E. DNA isolation and RAPD analysis:

1g  of  juvenile  leaves  of Meizotropis  pellita from  wild  and in vitro regenerated  plant  was  collected  and  DNA  was  isolated using CTAB method.

Based on RAPD profile DNA bands were scored. Presence and absence of individual bands were denoted as 1 and 0 respectively.  Monomorphic  loci  represent  the  no.  of  bands present in both the sample while polymorphic loci represent no. of unshared bands.

## D. Isolation of Genomic DNA:

Leaves of wild and in vitro raised Meizotropis  pellita were collected for the analysis. The DNA was isolated from both the samples separately and quality and quantity of DNA was assessed  by  agarose  gel  electrophoresis  (2%)  using  DNA double digest as marker and by calculating the ratio 260/280 nm absorbance to ensure the use of good quality of DNA for further  RAPD  assay.  CTAB  method  is  preferred  for  DNA extraction from  juvenile tissues as they contain lesser amount of polysaccharides, polyphenols and other secondary metabolites which obstructs the extraction process  as  they  form  co-precipitate  with  DNA,  and  thus hinders DNA digestion and PCR (Zhang and McStewart, 2000)  [5]. Precipitation  by  high  salt concentrations  for polysaccharide  elimination  was  worthwhile (Fang et  al ., 1992)  [6]. RAPD  reactions  can  be  adversely  affected  by polysaccharides. These biomolecules may deform the outcomes in many analytical executions and therefore, lead to false elucidation. Chloroform: Isoamyl alcohol, treatment is necessary for removal of  Cholorophyll and other coloring substances  such  as  pigments,  dyes  and  proteins.  RNA presence  during  DNA  isolation  can  minimize  the  yield  of PCR; treatment with  RNAase degrades RNA  leaving ultimately pure DNA behind.

## E. PCR Amplification:

10  random  primers  were  used  and  7  of  them  generated scorable  products  by  amplifying      the  template  DNA  with Taq Polymerase. The amplified products were separated on 2% agarose gel stained with Ethidium bromide. Scoring was done based on the presence or absence of bands as '1' and '0' respectively. ## F. RAPD Analysis:

DNA sequence  knowledge  and  species  specificity  are  not pre-requisites for RAPD analysis hence it can be employed for establishing genetic diversity and clonal fidelity among in  vitro raised  plantlets. ## IV CONCLUSION

Indian  subcontinent  has  one  of  the  oldest,  richest  and  most cultural  traditions  associated  with  the  use  of  natural  resources including vegetational wealth. In recent years, the due to developmental programmes urbanization, industrialization, overgrazing, animal husbandry tourism urbanization, industrialization, hydro-electric power, exploitation of economic plants, deforestation, global warming, climatic change there is a great loss of biological resources (Gaur 2007a, 2007b). [8] [9] Meizotropis  pellita ,  is  an  endangered  and  endemic  plant  of Uttarakhand.  Fire,  trampling,  human  interference,  disturbances in  ecology  due  to  heavy  industrialization,  global  warming  has led  to  general  degradation  of  natural  habitat  of  this  plant.  It belongs to the  family  Fabaceae  (earlier  Leguminosae),  so have potential  to  fix  atmospheric  nitrogen.  It  also  reported  to  have antioxidant and anti microbial property, which can be utilized, as potential  source for  drug  discovery .  [10]  [11] This  Indian Himalayan species is at the verge of extinction, so needs to be conserved to stop biodiversity loss.

In the present study in vitro regeneration protocol of Meizotropis pellita from seeds has been developed for large scale multiplication of this plant as  well as  this  protocol can also be used  for  raising  genetically  uniform  plants,  which  is  important for the sustainable supply of plant materials to the pharmaceutical industries and for conservation of elite germplasm. It is revealed that phytohormones like Auxins (2 - 4, D), Cytokinin and Gibberellic acid acid (BA and GA3) and IBA were  used  for  callus,  shoot  and  root  induction  for in  vitro propagation .[12] RAPD marker was used to evaluate the genetic stability and genetic fidelity among in vitro raised and wild type plant. The present protocol of in vitro propagation can further be used in developing  regeneration protocol for other similar endemic  and  endangered  plant,  which  require  urgent  need  for conservation, ultimately leading to biodiversity conservation.
